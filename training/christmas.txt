Santa opened the fridge and found a tiny snowman eating his cheese. "That is for Christmas dinner," said Santa. The snowman put on a paper crown. "Then I am the guest of honour." Santa offered him a carrot. The snowman stuck it on his face and asked for a mirror. By midnight, the cheese was gone and the fridge was wearing a little red hat. Santa left a note on the door: Please stop inviting the weather to dinner.

On Christmas Eve, Santa's beard floated away. It drifted across the workshop like a small white cloud. The elves tried to catch it with a fishing net, but it hid behind the moon. "Come back," called Santa. "You still have half my biscuit." The beard returned at once. Santa tied a ribbon around it and finished his tea. That year, every present contained one mysterious white hair and a very small apology.

The Christmas pudding grew legs and escaped from the kitchen. It ran past the tree, jumped into a boot, and declared itself king. Santa bowed politely. "Your Majesty, dinner is getting cold." The pudding demanded a tiny throne and six raisins in uniform. The elves brought a chair and a packet of raisins. By the time the royal parade was ready, everyone had eaten sandwiches. The pudding called this a great victory for pudding rights.

A reindeer walked into the workshop wearing twelve slippers. "My feet are cold," he explained. "You only have four feet," said an elf. The reindeer looked down in surprise. "Then whose are the other eight?" The slippers began to dance. Santa put them in charge of wrapping presents. They did a terrible job, but every parcel arrived with a lovely little tap dance. Nobody asked for a refund.

The snowman wanted a summer holiday for Christmas. Santa gave him a picture of a beach and a small fan. The snowman sat in front of the picture wearing sunglasses. "This is wonderful," he said. "The sea is so still." A penguin brought him an empty coconut. They spent the afternoon complaining about the imaginary heat. When a real snowflake landed on his nose, the snowman wrote a strongly worded postcard to the sun.

Santa's sleigh turned into a giant potato just before midnight. The reindeer refused to pull it without gravy. An elf added wheels, another added a bell, and Santa climbed into the middle. They rolled down the hill, leaving a trail of butter behind them. Every chimney smelled like lunch. In the morning, the children found presents beneath the tree and a tiny fork in each stocking. Santa called it his most delicious delivery yet.

An elf wrapped the wrong thing for Christmas. Instead of a toy train, he wrapped the sound of a duck. When the child opened the box, the room went quack. The tree went quack. Even the grandfather clock went quack. Santa tried to explain, but his beard went quack too. The child loved the present. For the rest of the day, the family told the time by counting ducks.

The Christmas tree refused to wear its decorations. "I would prefer trousers," it said. Grandma found a large green pair in the cupboard. The tree put them on and looked very pleased. Unfortunately, the trousers had six pockets, and squirrels moved into all of them. By dinner, the tree was collecting rent in nuts. Grandma hung one star on its highest branch. "A business tree still needs a little sparkle," she said.

On Christmas morning, a giant Brussels sprout knocked on the door. It was carrying flowers and wearing a purple tie. "I have come to apologise for last year's dinner," it said. Santa invited it inside. The sprout told excellent jokes and helped with the washing up. Nobody wanted to eat it after that. Instead, they gave it a stocking and a chair. It stayed until February, when it finally found a job as a very round librarian.

Santa lost his list and found a shopping receipt instead. He delivered six onions to a castle and a packet of soap to the moon. The moon was delighted. "I have been meaning to wash these craters," it said. The king was less excited about the onions until they began to sing. Santa decided the mistake had worked out rather well. Next Christmas, he borrowed a menu from a restaurant.

A gingerbread man became the village dentist. His first patient was a Christmas cracker with a loose joke. "Open wide," said the gingerbread man. The cracker shouted bang and covered the room in paper hats. The dentist fell into a cup of tea. He climbed out looking slightly softer but very professional. "You need fewer bad jokes and more floss," he said. The cracker promised to brush its punchlines twice a day.

The moon came down for Christmas dinner. It sat on the roof because it would not fit through the door. Santa passed plates of food up the chimney. The moon ate three puddings and became very round indeed. After dinner, it tried to return to the sky but got stuck behind a cloud. The reindeer gave it a gentle push. For a week, the night smelled faintly of custard.

Mrs Claus bought Santa a new pair of boots. Unfortunately, the boots were afraid of snow. They hid under the bed whenever the door opened. Santa spent Christmas Eve walking on his hands while the boots offered advice from a suitcase. "Watch that chimney," said the left boot. The right boot complained about the cold. Next year, Santa promised to buy a pair with less imagination.

The smallest elf invented a machine that wrapped feelings. He put happiness inside a red box and tied it with a ribbon. Then he accidentally wrapped his own confusion. Santa opened that present and spent ten minutes asking a chair for directions. The chair was surprisingly helpful. It pointed him toward the biscuits. Everyone agreed that confusion made a wonderful gift, provided it came with snacks.

A snowflake landed on Santa's desk and asked for a job. "I have excellent falling skills," it said. Santa made it head of winter. The snowflake immediately ordered more tea breaks and softer roofs. The other snowflakes were delighted. They floated down so slowly that Christmas arrived before the snow did. Santa had to decorate the garden with white socks. Nobody noticed until one of them sneezed.

The Christmas turkey announced that it was now a magician. It put a tablecloth over the dinner and shouted a very important word. When the cloth came off, all the vegetables were wearing tiny hats. Grandma applauded. Santa asked where the potatoes had gone. The turkey looked nervous. A small voice from inside the top hat said, "We are on holiday." Everyone ate toast and agreed that the show had been worth it.

Santa found a door inside his stocking. Behind the door was another Christmas, only much smaller. A tiny Santa was trying to deliver a tiny piano to a tiny elephant. "Do you need help?" asked the big Santa. "Only with the stairs," said the little one. Together they carried the piano. The elephant played one enormous note. Both Christmas trees fell over, and everyone laughed until the stockings shook.

A penguin arrived at the North Pole with a suitcase full of warm snow. The elves were suspicious. "Snow is supposed to be cold," they said. The penguin poured it into mugs and added marshmallows. It tasted like hot chocolate. Santa bought the whole suitcase. By evening, everyone was drinking snow and building snowmen out of biscuits. The penguin opened a shop called Weather You Can Eat.

Rudolph's nose began playing the trumpet. It played during breakfast, during lunch, and all the way through Santa's nap. An elf tried to turn it down, but only found a tiny volume knob behind an antler. At midnight, the nose played a cheerful Christmas tune. Every door opened at once. Santa finished his deliveries early, and Rudolph's nose received three invitations to join a band.

The presents under the tree held a meeting. The red box wanted a bigger ribbon. The blue box wanted to know what was inside itself. The smallest parcel demanded a chair. When the family came downstairs, all the gifts were standing in a line with serious little faces. Grandma gave them biscuits and promised to open them gently. The smallest parcel turned out to contain a chair. It was very satisfied.

An elf accidentally posted Christmas to the wrong address. It arrived at a quiet pond in the middle of July. The frogs put on paper crowns and sang carols until the fish complained. Santa came to collect the holiday, but the frogs asked to keep the pudding. He agreed. To this day, that pond celebrates Christmas whenever someone drops a biscuit into it.

Santa's hat decided it wanted to be a boat. It floated across the bath with three elves inside. They sailed past a rubber duck and discovered a new island made of soap. The elves planted a candy cane and declared it Christmas Island. Santa needed his hat back, so he drained the bath. The sailors called it a terrible storm. The rubber duck asked for a medal.

The Christmas tree grew a doorbell. Whenever someone pressed it, a tiny carol singer popped out of a branch. At first this was charming. Then the cat sat on the bell. Forty singers appeared and demanded tea. Grandma filled the bath with hot water and dropped in a giant tea bag. The singers were delighted. The cat was put in charge of silence, a job it took very seriously.

A lost cloud wandered into Santa's workshop. It was looking for the weather department. The elves decorated it with stars and hung it over the table. During Christmas dinner, it rained gravy onto the potatoes. Everyone applauded, except the pudding, which opened a little umbrella. Santa asked the cloud to come back next year. It said it would check its forecast.

The gingerbread house ordered a pizza. When the delivery elf arrived, the front door ate the receipt. "Sorry," said the house. "I get hungry around Christmas." The elf passed the pizza through a window. The chimney burped a cloud shaped like a mushroom. Santa thought the house looked much happier. He left a very large toothbrush beside the gate.

On Christmas Eve, every clock in the village turned into a chicken. Nobody knew what time it was, but everyone had plenty of eggs. Santa asked the oldest chicken when midnight would arrive. "After eleven," it said, sounding offended. The elves put the eggs into stockings. In the morning, the village enjoyed a Christmas breakfast that was exactly on time, although no one could prove it.

The snowman found a pair of tap shoes beneath the tree. He danced across the frozen pond with a penguin orchestra. His carrot nose kept the beat. Then one of his coal eyes fell out and rolled into the piano. The music became much darker. The snowman bowed and called it jazz. Santa gave him a scarf and asked him to stop dancing on the roof.

Santa tried to teach a spoon to fly. The spoon was very eager but kept landing in soup. The elves built it a tiny sleigh and attached two marshmallows. At last it flew across the kitchen and delivered a pea to every plate. "Excellent work," said Santa. The spoon asked for a promotion. By New Year's Day, it was head of very small deliveries.

A Christmas cracker contained an entire orchestra. When Dad pulled it, twelve musicians and a confused conductor landed on the table. The conductor climbed onto the pudding and raised his spoon. They played a cheerful tune while everyone ate. The trumpet player got stuck in a bread roll. Grandma said it was the best cracker she had ever bought, although the washing up was going to be terrible.

The North Pole moved three steps to the left while Santa was asleep. His workshop woke up in the sea. The elves put buckets on their heads and continued wrapping presents. A whale offered to pull the sleigh. It was very strong but kept stopping to sing. Santa delivered every present with a splash. The children found seaweed in their stockings and thought it was a new kind of scarf.

A tiny dragon wanted to be a Christmas candle. It stood on the table and breathed a very small flame. The turkey wore a fireman's helmet just in case. Everything went well until the dragon sneezed. The paper crowns turned into toast. Grandma spread them with butter and called it a new tradition. The dragon was invited back, but only after practising its quiet sneezes.

The star on the Christmas tree asked for a day off. Santa replaced it with a fried egg. The egg shone beautifully until the cat noticed. After a long discussion, the cat agreed to leave it alone in exchange for a sausage ornament. The tree smelled like breakfast all afternoon. The star returned in the evening wearing sunglasses and carrying a tiny postcard from the moon.

Mrs Claus found Santa stuck inside a Christmas card. He was waving from a snowy picture and asking for help. She opened the card, and he fell out with three paper reindeer. "Next time, use the door," she said. Santa brushed glitter from his beard. The paper reindeer stayed for dinner. They ate the napkins and said the meal was wonderfully light.

A very serious potato inspected Santa's sleigh. It wore glasses and carried a clipboard. "There are too many bells," it announced. Santa asked how many bells were allowed. The potato counted slowly and fell asleep at seven. The elves tucked it into a stocking. Santa flew away with all his bells ringing. In the morning, the potato woke up and approved the pillow instead.

The Christmas socks escaped from the washing line. They marched through the village demanding better feet. The mayor offered them hot chocolate and a pair of slippers. The socks were not impressed. Then Santa arrived with a dancing octopus. It had eight warm arms and excellent manners. The socks climbed aboard and spent Christmas showing off their new owner.

Santa gave the wind a scarf. The wind wrapped it around the village, tied a bow on the church, and accidentally pulled the snow off every roof. The elves had to put it all back with teaspoons. "A little smaller next time," Santa suggested. The wind blushed and blew a gentle kiss. Everyone's paper crown flew into the soup.

A talking mince pie wanted to become an astronaut. Santa gave it a tiny helmet and a rocket made from a Christmas cracker. The pie landed on the moon just before dinner. It planted a little flag and discovered that the moon was not made of cheese. "Very disappointing," it wrote in its report. It returned home with a pocket full of stars and asked for custard.

The reindeer formed a choir, but they only knew the word potato. They sang it softly, loudly, and with great feeling. Santa listened with tears in his eyes. "Beautiful," he said. "But perhaps one more word?" The reindeer thought for a long time. On Christmas Eve, they added gravy. The elves gave them a standing ovation and a very large dinner plate.

A Christmas bell lost its ring. It searched under the sofa, inside the pudding, and behind the moon. At last it found the ring on a tiny mouse's finger. The mouse had married a biscuit. Santa congratulated the happy couple and gave the bell a bicycle horn. It sounded strange during the carols, but nobody could say the wedding had been dull.

Grandma knitted a Christmas jumper for the whole village. It covered the houses, the church, and most of the pond. Everyone was warm, but the buses could not find the sleeves. Santa landed on the collar and delivered presents through the buttons. The village looked wonderful from the sky. In January, it took forty people and one very patient crane to fold the jumper.

An elf sneezed so hard that Tuesday became Christmas. Everyone was delighted except Wednesday, which had already made plans. Santa held an emergency meeting with the calendar. They agreed to move lunch forward and leave the weekend alone. The elf apologised into a tissue. The tissue turned into Easter. Santa decided it was time to buy better tissues.

The snowman borrowed Santa's beard for a party. He looked so important that everyone asked him for presents. He gave them snowballs, cold hugs, and a careful explanation of winter. A child asked for a pony. The snowman made one from snow. It galloped around the garden until spring, when it became a surprisingly fast puddle.

Santa's toy train grew bigger every time someone said Christmas. Soon it filled the workshop. Then it filled the village. The elves had to whisper. Santa climbed into the engine and drove it around the moon. He delivered presents to every cloud along the way. When he returned, Mrs Claus said, "Merry Christmas!" The train became a railway station. Santa went to bed in the waiting room.

A Christmas pudding applied to become mayor. Its promises included more cream, fewer forks, and free raisins for everyone. The village voted for it at once. On its first day, the pudding banned washing up. On its second day, it hid from a spoon. By the third day, everyone agreed it was doing about as well as the last mayor, but it smelled much nicer.

The elves discovered a hole in Christmas. It was just behind the wrapping paper. Through the hole they could see next year's dinner. Santa reached inside and borrowed a potato. Mrs Claus told him to put it back. "We will need that," she said. He returned it with a tiny apology note. Next Christmas, the potato read the note aloud and accepted the apology.

A reindeer brought a ladder to Christmas dinner. "The conversation is too high for me," he explained. He climbed to the top and discussed the weather with the star on the tree. They became good friends. When dessert arrived, the reindeer climbed down with a cloud in his antlers. It rained cream over the pudding. Everyone agreed that the ladder had been an excellent idea.

Santa's sack was full of doors. Every time he opened one, he found another door with a smaller Santa behind it. The elves lined them up across the workshop. By midnight, there were hundreds of Santas, each carrying one present. Delivery took only six minutes. Unfortunately, Christmas breakfast required hundreds of tiny cups of tea. The washing up lasted until March.

A lonely snowball wanted a Christmas hug. It rolled into the village and collected every scarf it could find. Soon it was enormous and very fashionable. The children climbed onto it and wrapped their arms around the snow. The snowball was so happy that it rolled down the hill. Everyone arrived at Christmas dinner slightly dizzy and wearing the same scarf.

The Christmas lights began telling jokes. The red bulb was funny, the green bulb was rude about vegetables, and the blue bulb kept forgetting the ending. Santa laughed so hard that his hat fell into the soup. The lights took a bow and went out. Dinner continued by candlelight. The candle said it had a joke too, but everyone begged it to remain a little brighter.

A penguin wrapped the sun as a Christmas present. It used all the paper in the world and a ribbon longer than the sea. The morning was very dark. Santa found the sun under an enormous tree and carefully opened one corner. Light poured out. "Lovely gift," he said, "but we should probably share it." The penguin nodded and kept the ribbon for a scarf.

The village chimney inspector was a very small giraffe. He wore a red hat and travelled in Santa's pocket. Every time he looked up a chimney, he found another giraffe looking down. "This one is occupied," he announced. Santa delivered the presents through the window instead. By morning, every roof had a little giraffe on it, and the village had no trouble reaching the Christmas lights.

A gingerbread man built himself a gingerbread house. When he got hungry, he ate the front step. Then he ate the porch. By Christmas Eve, only the bathroom remained. Santa found him sitting in the bath with a cup of tea. "I may have made a small design mistake," said the man. Santa gave him a wooden house and a very large sandwich.

Santa's chair began laying presents like eggs. Each morning, a new parcel appeared beneath it. The elves fed it cushions and spoke to it kindly. On Christmas Eve, it produced a grand piano. Santa was impressed, but the chair looked exhausted. Mrs Claus made it a cup of tea. The chair sat down on another chair, and everyone decided not to ask any questions.

A snowman opened a bakery at the North Pole. He sold snow cakes, snow bread, and very cold buns. The elves complained that everything tasted like winter. The snowman added one raisin to each bun and doubled the price. Santa bought a dozen. "Excellent business," he said. By spring, the bakery had become a swimming pool, which was also very popular.

The Christmas tree sneezed and all its decorations flew into space. The moon caught a red bauble and put it on its ear. The stars borrowed the tinsel. Santa asked them to return everything before dinner. They agreed, except for one tiny star that had fallen in love with a candy cane. Grandma said it could stay. The tree wore a paper crown and pretended not to mind.

An elf made a toy telephone that only called vegetables. Santa dialled the workshop and reached a worried cabbage. He called Mrs Claus and got a carrot with a terrible cold. At last he tried the number on a Christmas card. A potato answered and offered him a job. Santa politely declined. He was already busy enough with one sack of things that needed delivering.

The Christmas snow fell upward. It rose from the garden, passed the windows, and settled on the underside of a cloud. The children built upside-down snowmen by hanging from the swings. Santa delivered presents from beneath his sleigh. Mrs Claus attached the pudding to the ceiling. Everyone agreed the day was very festive, although eating custard required unusual confidence.

A mouse stole Santa's enormous hat. It dragged the hat into its tiny house and invited every mouse in the village. They held a Christmas party inside it. Santa arrived looking for his hat and heard music from the floor. He tapped politely on the brim. The mice invited him in. He could only fit one finger, but they gave it a paper crown and a piece of cheese.

A reindeer forgot how to land. It flew around the North Pole all morning, waving politely at everyone. Santa held up a sign that said Down Is That Way. The reindeer read it upside down and flew higher. At last Mrs Claus put a biscuit on the roof. The reindeer landed perfectly. "I remember now," it said. Santa decided to include biscuits in the flight manual.

The Christmas calendar refused to stop at December. It invented a new month called Pudding. During Pudding, every day was a holiday and all the rain tasted of custard. The elves enjoyed themselves until the calendar demanded a birthday party. Santa bought it a cake with thirty-one candles. The calendar blew them out and turned back into January. Everyone was relieved, especially the dentist.

Santa found a whale in the bathtub wearing a Christmas jumper. "I thought this was the North Pool," said the whale. Santa explained the mistake and offered it a biscuit. The whale was too large to leave through the door, so the elves gave the bath wheels. It pulled the sleigh across the sky that night. Every present arrived clean, damp, and wrapped in a lovely bubble.

The Christmas angel on top of the tree wanted to be a plumber. She climbed down with a tiny spanner and fixed the dripping tap. Then she repaired the kettle, the shower, and a cloud outside. The village had perfect weather for a week. Santa asked her to return to the tree for dinner. "After this radiator," she said. Nobody had the heart to interrupt an angel with a useful hobby.

A Christmas card grew teeth and ate the postman's sandwich. The postman complained to Santa. Santa gave the card a little lunchbox and taught it to say please. It became very polite, although it still chewed the occasional stamp. By New Year, it had sent itself to Grandma. She fed it soup and used it to open stubborn envelopes.

The smallest present contained a very large silence. When it was opened, the carols stopped, the bells went quiet, and the cat could not meow. Everyone looked around in surprise. Then Grandpa dropped a spoon. The silence broke into a thousand tiny giggles. They floated around the room all afternoon. Santa said it was an excellent present, although he wished someone had kept the receipt.

A snowman made a wish on a Christmas star. He wished for a nose that could smell biscuits. The star gave him a carrot with a little bell on it. Whenever biscuits were nearby, the bell rang. It rang all through Santa's workshop. It rang beside the tree. It rang in Grandma's handbag. The snowman spent a very happy Christmas following his nose into places where noses rarely belonged.

The Christmas pudding learned to drive. It borrowed Santa's sleigh and took the custard to the seaside. The reindeer were left behind with a map and a worried expression. Santa followed the trail of raisins. He found the pudding parked beside the beach, wearing sunglasses. "Dinner is at six," he said. The pudding promised to be home, provided nobody brought a spoon.

An elf painted a door on the wall. Santa opened it and stepped into a room full of Tuesdays. They were all wearing Christmas hats and arguing about the weather. Santa invited them to dinner. By evening, the table was crowded with Tuesdays, and Wednesday was furious. Mrs Claus gave everyone a different coloured plate. It did not solve the calendar, but it made washing up easier.

A reindeer ordered new antlers from a catalogue. The parcel contained two tiny Christmas trees. He put them on anyway. Birds moved in, bells rang when he walked, and a squirrel opened a shop above his left ear. Santa thought he looked magnificent. The reindeer agreed, but asked whether anyone knew how to turn off the lights before bedtime.

Grandpa's Christmas jumper started telling stories. Its knitted snowman described a journey to the moon, and its woolly reindeer explained how to cook clouds. Everyone listened carefully. Then the jumper reached the end of its yarn. Grandpa had to stand very still while Grandma knitted the final chapter. The story ended with a wedding between a mitten and a teapot. Nobody had expected that.

The North Pole was delivered a bill for all its snow. Santa called the clouds to complain. A polite cloud explained that the first thousand flakes were free. The rest cost one biscuit each. Santa offered a plate of mince pies instead. The clouds held a meeting and agreed. That winter, the snow smelled of cinnamon, and the forecast was unusually cheerful.

The Christmas fairy turned a pumpkin into a taxi. It drove Santa around the village while the reindeer took a tea break. The taxi refused to use roads and bounced through the gardens instead. Every passenger received a free seed. At midnight, it turned back into a pumpkin. Santa paid the fare with a spoon and walked home carrying the driver in a soup bowl.

A gingerbread man joined the fire brigade. His first job was to rescue a Christmas candle from a tree. He climbed a ladder with a tiny bucket of milk. The candle thanked him and asked for a blanket. Unfortunately, the gingerbread man slipped into the bucket. He emerged looking rather soft. Santa gave him a medal and suggested a career with fewer liquids.

Santa's workshop grew a second floor made entirely of jelly. The elves bounced upstairs and wrapped presents while wobbling gently. Every time someone dropped a toy, the whole building said boing. Santa tried to make an important speech, but bounced through the ceiling halfway through. Mrs Claus caught him with a laundry basket. They decided the new floor would be used only for dancing.

A lonely Christmas cracker asked Santa for a friend. Santa gave it a balloon. They spent the afternoon discussing the dangers of sudden excitement. Then a hedgehog arrived wearing a party hat. Both friends hid behind the pudding. The hedgehog was very polite and promised to stand still. They had a wonderful Christmas, although nobody suggested a group hug.

The village snowman received a heated blanket for Christmas. He read the instructions very carefully. Then he gave the blanket to a cold stone and kept the box. The stone was delighted. The snowman decorated the box and called it a holiday cottage. Everyone admired his sensible decision. Santa quietly crossed electric toaster off next year's list.

Santa opened a present and found his own laughter inside. It jumped out and bounced around the room, knocking hats off the elves. Santa tried to catch it, but every attempt made him laugh harder. At last Mrs Claus put a biscuit on a plate. The laughter settled down to eat. Santa swallowed it with his tea and immediately told a joke about a very confused chimney.

The reindeer asked for a swimming pool at Christmas. Santa filled a huge bowl with warm water. They climbed in with their antlers sticking out like little trees. The elves decorated the antlers with floating candles. A duck arrived and declared itself lifeguard. Nobody could swim, but everyone enjoyed standing in the water and looking important. The duck blew its whistle whenever someone splashed the pudding.

A Christmas ornament discovered that it was a planet. It had three tiny moons and a village of glittering people. Santa held it carefully while the villagers waved. Their mayor asked for a larger tree next year. Santa promised to try. Then a cat tapped the planet with one paw. The villagers experienced their first earthquake and immediately invented insurance.

The Christmas stockings began snoring before anyone went to bed. They were exhausted from holding so many presents. Santa put little pillows beneath them and delivered the remaining toys into shoes. One boot received a bicycle and was never quite the same again. In the morning, the stockings woke up refreshed. They asked for breakfast and refused to discuss the terrible things they had smelled.

An elf invented invisible wrapping paper. He wrapped every present in the workshop and then lost them all. Santa spent an hour feeling around under the table. Mrs Claus sprinkled flour over the floor, revealing a bicycle, a rocking horse, and a very embarrassed elf. The presents were finally delivered with a thin white coating. The children called them snow presents and licked the bicycles just to be sure.

Santa's beard became a small forest. Birds nested beside his chin, mushrooms grew near his collar, and a squirrel rented his left eyebrow. Mrs Claus watered him every morning. On Christmas Eve, the elves decorated the beard with lights. Santa looked wonderful, but could no longer drink tea without disturbing a family of owls. He bought a very long straw and learned to sip quietly.

A Christmas biscuit asked the moon to dance. The moon came down wearing silver shoes, and they spun around the garden. The stars clapped from above. Santa played the accordion until one of the keys turned into a fish. The biscuit bowed and thanked everyone. It returned to the plate just before midnight, looking slightly dizzy and covered in moon dust.

The Christmas postman delivered a parcel full of yesterday. Santa opened it and found the breakfast he had already eaten. The elves found their old mistakes and tried to correct them. Mrs Claus found a cup of tea that was finally the right temperature. They enjoyed yesterday so much that they nearly forgot tomorrow. Santa packed the remaining hours into a stocking and labelled it For Later.

A snowman became a detective after losing his carrot. He questioned the rabbit, the gardener, and a suspicious bowl of soup. Everyone denied taking it. At last he looked in the mirror. The carrot was still on his face. "A clever disguise," he declared. Santa gave him a magnifying glass for Christmas. The snowman immediately opened a new case about the enormous eye inside it.

The Christmas tree wanted to go ice skating. The elves tied skates to its roots and pushed it onto the pond. It spun beautifully, scattering tinsel across the ice. A squirrel clung to a branch and shouted directions. Santa gave the tree ten points for style and two points for stopping. They finally caught it in a snowdrift. It asked to try skiing next year.

A giant mitten arrived at Christmas dinner without its partner. "We had a falling out," it explained. Grandma poured it tea and listened kindly. Later, the other mitten knocked on the window carrying flowers. They made up beside the fire. Everyone applauded, except the gloves, who thought the whole thing was terribly dramatic. Santa gave the mittens a shared scarf and a very small holiday.

Santa accidentally gave the cat a magic wand. The cat turned the Christmas tree into a fish and the sofa into a larger fish. Then it turned Santa into a man carrying fish, which was almost normal. Mrs Claus traded the wand for a cardboard box. The cat was delighted. It spent the rest of Christmas sitting in the box and ignoring its magnificent fish furniture.

The North Pole lost its north. The sign simply said Pole, and all the compasses pointed at lunch. Santa followed one to a sandwich shop. Behind the counter stood north itself, wearing an apron. "I needed a change of direction," it said. Santa ordered two sandwiches and offered it shorter hours. North agreed to return after Christmas. Until then, everyone navigated by the smell of soup.

A Christmas pudding fell in love with a custard jug. It wrote poems on little paper napkins and sent flowers made of raisins. The jug blushed a pale yellow. Santa offered to conduct the wedding, but the spoons looked suspiciously hungry. The couple ran away on a biscuit. They settled in a quiet cupboard and sent everyone a postcard that simply said Please Do Not Stir.

The Christmas dinner table grew longer whenever someone told a lie. Grandpa said he had eaten only one biscuit, and the table stretched through the door. Santa said he had finished all his work, and it reached the next village. Mrs Claus told the truth about the washing up. The table became very small indeed. They ate dinner from their knees and agreed to be more careful with conversation.

An elf invented a toy that did absolutely nothing. Santa examined it for a long time. "How does it work?" he asked. "Perfectly," said the elf. The toy became the most popular Christmas present in the world. It never broke, never needed batteries, and never woke anyone at six in the morning. The elf won a prize, which was also nothing. He was very proud of it.

Santa found a polite monster inside the chimney. It had six eyes, three hats, and a small cup of tea. "I am waiting for Christmas," it explained. Santa pointed out that it was blocking the presents. The monster apologised and folded itself into a stocking. In the morning, the children found six eyes blinking beside the oranges. They gave the monster a name and asked it to help with the difficult wrapping paper.

The Christmas choir swallowed a snowstorm. Whenever they sang, tiny snowflakes came out of their mouths. The village looked beautiful, but the piano kept freezing. Santa gave the singers hot chocolate. Their next song produced marshmallows instead. The children stood in front with bowls. The concert was sold out, although half the audience had only come for dessert.

The moon sent Santa a thank-you letter for the soap. It said the craters were spotless and the stars had noticed. Santa was pleased until he saw the parcel attached to the letter. The moon had returned all its dirty water. The elves carried buckets for three days. Mrs Claus watered the Christmas tree with the last bucket. It grew one small silver moon instead of a star.

A gingerbread man bought a suit made of icing. He looked very smart at the Christmas party. Unfortunately, everyone kept asking whether they could have a taste. He put on a sign saying Please Compliment The Tailoring. Santa admired the buttons. Grandma praised the collar. The gingerbread man had a wonderful evening until he sat too close to the fire and became a very elegant puddle.

Santa's shadow refused to work on Christmas Eve. It wanted to stay home and watch a film. Santa borrowed the shadow of a giraffe instead. Every roof he crossed looked much taller than usual. The reindeer thought he had grown a very long neck. Santa delivered all the presents without bending down. In the morning, he returned the shadow with a thank-you note and a scarf that was six metres long.

The Christmas tree's fairy lights began growing pears. Grandma picked them carefully and made a glowing pie. Every slice lit up the room. Santa ate two pieces and could see through his own beard. The elves turned off the lamps to save electricity. By midnight, everyone at the dinner table was gently shining. The cat slept underneath them and dreamed of warm stars.

An elf accidentally wrapped the front door. Nobody could leave the workshop until Christmas morning. Santa tried the windows, but they had been wrapped too. At last Mrs Claus found an unwrapped chimney. They climbed out one by one, carrying the presents. The elf apologised. Santa gave him a new job wrapping sandwiches, where his enthusiasm caused fewer problems.

A snowman wanted to learn the violin. Santa gave him one made of ice. He played a sad song about a carrot that had moved away. The village cried so much that the pond became a little deeper. Then he played a cheerful song about socks. Everyone danced, including the pond. Santa asked him to finish with something quiet before the river joined in.

The reindeer found a map of Christmas inside a biscuit. It showed the workshop, the moon, and a secret cupboard full of gravy. They followed the crumbs across the snow. Behind a tiny door, they found Santa eating a sandwich in peace. He looked very surprised. The reindeer promised to keep the secret. In return, Santa let them draw a swimming pool on next year's map.

The Christmas star fell into a bowl of soup. It bobbed between the carrots, looking embarrassed. Grandma lifted it out with a spoon and dried it on a napkin. "You taste of wishes," she said after trying the soup. Everyone made a wish before dinner. The cat wished for fish, Grandpa wished for a nap, and the soup wished to be a famous ocean. Santa quietly moved it to a larger bowl.

Santa woke up with bells instead of buttons. Every time he breathed, his coat played a tune. The elves danced while they packed the sleigh. Mrs Claus tried to tell him something important, but he sneezed and started the chorus. By midnight, the reindeer knew the whole song. Santa decided it was a wonderful coat for Christmas and a terrible coat for visiting the library.

A giant Christmas present arrived without a label. Inside was another present, and inside that was a smaller one. The family kept opening boxes until they found a tiny note. It said Thank You For Unpacking My House. A mouse came out, hung a wreath on the biggest box, and invited everyone for tea. Grandma said it was the most polite property development she had ever seen.

The snow outside Santa's workshop began spelling words. First it wrote Hello. Then it wrote More Biscuits Please. Santa left a plate on the step. The snow ate everything and wrote Thank You in very neat letters. The elves asked it to write the Christmas cards. It did a beautiful job, but every envelope melted before it reached the post office. Santa called it a promising first draft.

Mrs Claus bought a Christmas tree from a travelling octopus. The tree had eight branches and liked to wave. It decorated itself, served the dinner, and washed all the plates. Santa thought it was wonderful. Then it asked for a salary and a seaside holiday. Mrs Claus agreed immediately. By New Year, the octopus had sold them a wardrobe that did the ironing and a carpet that walked the dog.
