The Christmas wreath rang the doorbell from the wrong side of the door. It wanted to spend the evening indoors. Anna took it off its hook and placed it beside the fire. The wreath stretched its leaves and asked whether anyone had saved it a piece of cake.

Anna brought a small plate, but the wreath had no hands. Her brother suggested hanging the cake from a ribbon. This worked until a robin landed on the ribbon and claimed that the cake was part of its nest. The wreath asked everyone to remain calm.

They divided the cake into three pieces. The robin carried one away, Anna ate another, and the wreath wore the last one like a hat. It returned to the door after dinner. The next visitor said that the house smelled wonderfully festive, although the door appeared to have been iced.

A bell lost its ring on Christmas morning. It shook itself as hard as it could, but only a small cough came out. An elf searched under the table and found a round silver sound hiding beside a boot. The sound said that it was tired of waking people up.

The elf offered it a quieter job inside a music box. The sound agreed to try. When the box opened, it made a beautiful gentle note. The bell listened and realised that it wanted a different job too. Santa suggested that it could hold a little flower.

By dinner, the music box had a new voice and the bell had a daisy. Neither had to wake anyone. The elf used a saucepan to call the reindeer instead. They were so surprised that they arrived early, carrying spoons and hoping that Christmas had finally turned into soup.

The North Pole library received a book with no pages. Inside its cover was a tiny snowy field. The librarian stepped into it to look for the missing words. She found them skating on a frozen comma near the middle of the field.

The words wanted a Christmas holiday. The librarian offered them one afternoon, provided they returned in the right order. Most agreed. The word backwards insisted on arriving before it had left, which made the timetable difficult. The librarian gave it a separate ticket.

When the cover closed, the field folded into white pages. Every word found its place except backwards, which appeared on the last page facing the wrong way. The librarian left it there. Children thought the ending was excellent, especially when they read the book in a mirror.

A parcel arrived for the village's oldest chimney. Inside was a woolly scarf long enough to reach the pavement. The chimney had always complained about cold nights, but nobody had realised that it could read letters. It sent a thank-you note down the fireplace.

The family wrapped the scarf around the bricks. Smoke began to come out in soft coloured stripes. A neighbour thought the house was knitting a cloud. Soon people were standing outside with empty baskets, hoping to catch a warm piece of sky.

The chimney made one striped cloud for everyone. The family hung the remaining scarf in the hall, where it dried politely without dripping soot. On Christmas night, Santa found the chimney much easier to recognise. He left it a pair of gloves, although nobody knew where it kept its hands.
