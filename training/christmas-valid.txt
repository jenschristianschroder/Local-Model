Santa discovered that his sleigh had become a giant teacup. The reindeer stared at it while an elf searched for the handle. Mrs Claus poured in warm tea and told everyone to climb aboard. They sailed over the village, leaving a trail of steam. Every child received a present and a biscuit. Santa called it a successful delivery, although he had never needed so much sugar before.

A snowman found a tiny piano beneath the Christmas tree. He played it with his carrot nose. The music made the snowflakes dance in circles, and soon the whole garden was spinning. Santa asked for a quieter song. The snowman played one small note, and a single snowflake sat down. Everyone agreed that this was excellent progress.

The Christmas pudding wore a crown and demanded a holiday. Grandma offered it a trip to the cupboard. The pudding wanted somewhere with a beach. Santa drew a sea on a plate and added an umbrella. The pudding sat beneath it, looking very pleased. Nobody mentioned the spoon waiting just beyond the horizon.

An elf invented a toy duck that could only say Christmas. It said Christmas to the kettle, the reindeer, and a very surprised potato. Santa took it on his deliveries. The duck shouted all night until the moon finally answered Happy Christmas. The elf was delighted. The potato remained confused.

A reindeer found a pair of enormous red trousers in Santa's sack. He put them on his antlers and called them a roof. Three birds moved in before lunch. By Christmas dinner, he was carrying a tiny village above his head. Santa gave the birds their presents first because they were closest to the sky.

The star on the Christmas tree opened a little cafe. It served hot chocolate to passing clouds and charged one snowflake per cup. The tree became very busy. Grandma had to climb a ladder to order dessert. Santa gave the cafe an excellent review, although he said the stairs were a little difficult for reindeer.

A gingerbread woman lost her house in a cup of tea. She watched it sink slowly and asked Santa for a boat. He gave her a teaspoon. She paddled across the cup and rescued her furniture, which was mostly sugar. By evening, she had moved into a biscuit tin. She said the new house had much better weather.

On Christmas morning, the presents began asking for presents. The family was confused. Grandma wrapped a ribbon for the red box and a small bow for the blue one. The boxes were delighted and hugged each other. Santa watched from the window and decided that Christmas was becoming much more complicated than he remembered.

The North Pole received a parcel containing a very small sea. Santa poured it into a bowl and added a toy boat. A tiny whale appeared and asked for Christmas pudding. The elves found a crumb and lowered it carefully into the water. The whale sang a grateful song, and the bowl sailed gently toward the fireplace.

Mrs Claus gave the wind a pair of mittens. It put them on and immediately stopped blowing through everyone's windows. The village became warm and quiet. Then the wind tried to clap along with a Christmas song. Both mittens flew into the chimney. Santa caught them in his beard and asked for a little less applause.

The Christmas turkey took a job as a librarian. It hid behind a large book whenever anyone mentioned dinner. Santa borrowed a story about a flying carrot. The turkey stamped the book and wished him a peaceful holiday. Everyone ate vegetable soup, and the turkey finally stopped looking at the oven with suspicion.

A tiny Christmas bell wanted to become a detective. It searched the workshop for the missing biscuits. The trail led to Santa's chair, where it found crumbs and a sleeping beard. The bell rang loudly. Santa woke up and offered it a biscuit. The detective closed the case, explaining that important evidence should never be wasted.
