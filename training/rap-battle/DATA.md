# Rap Battle training data

The examples in `corpus.json` (Laptop and Cloud), `robot-toaster.json` (Robot and Toaster), and `expanded-roster.json` (the ten additional opponents) were authored for this demo. The additional verses in `pigeon-variety.json` were also written for this demo. The examples and their synthetic combinations are dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). No external lyrics, other datasets, or pretrained checkpoints enter the training run.

The original four models were trained in pairs. The ten expansion models were initially trained from random initialization using only `expanded-roster.json`. Each expansion model learns its own replies in the context of verses from the other nine new opponents. You can mix all 14 opponents in the browser. The original models may still reference their training partners. Pigeon was subsequently retrained from random initialization on `pigeon-variety.json`, which contains 40 new Pigeon training couplets and newly written context from four other opponents.

The trainer pairs a previous verse with one of its words and a two-line response written for the other performer. It also trains opening verses. These combinations are generated offline. The response target uses a dedicated copy token where the callback word belongs. At inference, that token renders as the selected word. The model learns when to emit it and samples the surrounding verse from its weights.

Validation uses separate opponent verses and response/callback pairings excluded from training. This tests transfer to new contexts and recombinations of familiar writing patterns. The corpus is deliberately small, and a model can reproduce training lines or miss the selected callback.

The shared challenge model was trained from random initialization using only the newly written `challenges.json` corpus. It uses a performer token to generate a two-line challenge in that character’s voice. The 84 training couplets and 14 held-out couplets are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0. Reproduce its checkpoint with `python training/rap-battle/train.py challenge`.

The shared reply checkpoint is trained from random initialization using only the newly written CC0 `context-corpus.txt`. Each of the 14 opponents has four opening couplets and five different topic-specific challenges with two rebuttals each. Training varies the selected callback and uses shortened or reordered versions of each challenge with its two rebuttals. When a known topic appears in the opponent’s challenge, the browser passes it as an additional prompt token. The validation reported by the trainer checks a shortened version of the same topics. It measures robustness to missing context and is not a held-out test of new verse quality. Reproduce with `python training/rap-battle/train.py context-reply`.
